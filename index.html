A Guide to the Population Standard Deviation

The population standard deviation conveys how widely the scores in a population are spread apart. The further each score is from the mean, the larger the resulting standard deviation. For this example, we'll look at the number of shows watched by four students (these values can be changed in the table).

This demonstration consists of four sections: Six Steps for Calculating the Population Standard Deviation, A Table to Organize the Numbers, The Visual Graph, and The Formula for the Population Standard Deviation.

Six Steps for Calculating the Population Standard Deviation

Click ‘Walk through it’, then ‘Next’, to take the steps one at a time. Each step highlights the exact values it produces in the table, so you can connect what the step says with what it calculates.

  1. Scores: 1, 4, 6, 5

  2. Mean = 4.00

  3. Difference scores: (1 − 4), (4 − 4), (6 − 4), (5 − 4)

    The difference scores always add to zero, with the plusses and minuses canceling each other out.

  4. Squared difference scores: (1 − 4)², (4 − 4)², (6 − 4)², (5 − 4)²

  5. Variance = 3.50

  6. Standard deviation = 1.87

    Why the square root?

    Squaring in step 4 left the variance in “squared units”. For example, the variance of 10, 20, and 30 is 66.67, which is larger than all of the values combined. The square root shrinks the answer back down to the original units. The standard deviation of 10, 20, and 30 is 8.16 (much closer to the original values).

The Formula for the Population Standard Deviation

The formula for the population standard deviation is like a baking recipe. It tells us the steps to follow for a successful outcome. The formula uses symbols rather than words, so that it can be written concisely. On the left side of the equal sign is the lowercase sigma, which looks like the letter ‘o’ with a small tail on its top extending slightly to the right. The lowercase sigma represents the population standard deviation.

σ= Σ(xi − μ)² N

On the right side of the equal sign are those same six steps covered previously, written using symbols.

Step 1: First, notice the “xi”. The x sub i is a lowercase x with a small lowercase i at its bottom right. It will be used to represent each score in our population (1, 4, 6, 5).

Step 2: Notice the μ, which looks like a lowercase u with a tail hanging down on its left side. It is the lowercase Greek letter mu, and represents the population mean.

Step 3: The formula tells us to subtract the mean from the score, written as “xi − μ”.

Step 4: Observe the small 2 just outside the parentheses: “(xi − μ)²”. It tells us to square the difference score.

Step 5: To the left of those parentheses sits the uppercase sigma “Σ”. It looks like a fancy uppercase E, and it tells us to ‘add them all up’. So, all of the squared difference scores will be added together.

Step 5, continued: All of this sits above a horizontal line, and below is an uppercase N, telling us to divide the sum of the squared difference scores by N (the population size). At this point, we've calculated the average of the squared difference scores, known as the population variance.

Step 6: Notice the square root sign, a check mark with a long horizontal arm running off to the right. It encompasses our equation, telling us that the population standard deviation (lowercase sigma) equals the square root of the population variance (the average of the squared difference scores).

How to read the formula aloud
σ= Σ(xi − μ)² N

Sigma equals the square root of the quantity, the sum of open parenthesis x sub i minus mu close parenthesis squared, divided by N.

This is a conceptual math formula that mirrors the logic presented in the ‘Six Steps for Calculating the Population Standard Deviation’ section. A computational formula also exists, reaching the same answer by a shortcut that is quicker to work by hand but hides the reasoning.

Note, when people simply say ‘sigma’ they generally mean the lowercase sigma (letter ‘o’ with the tail at the top right), representing the population standard deviation.
