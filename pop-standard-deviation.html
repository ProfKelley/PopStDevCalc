Standard Deviation: A Step By Step Guide

The Standard Deviation conveys how widely the scores are spread apart. A value of zero indicates that every score is identical. The larger the standard deviation, the greater the spread of scores. For this example, we'll look at the number of shows watched by four students (in The Numbers section, these values can be changed). We will then go through the six steps for calculating the standard deviation.

This demonstration consists of four sections: The Six Steps, The Numbers, The Visual Graph, and The Formula for the Population Standard Deviation.

The Six Steps

Click ‘Walk through it’, then ‘Next’, to take the steps one at a time. Each step highlights the exact values it produces in The Numbers section, so you can connect what the step says with what it calculates.

  1. Values: 1, 4, 6, 5

  2. Mean = 4.00

  3. Difference scores: (1 − 4.00), (4 − 4.00), (6 − 4.00), (5 − 4.00)

  4. Squared difference scores: 9.00, 0.00, 4.00, 1.00

    The difference scores always add to zero, with the plusses and minuses canceling each other out. By squaring each difference score, this issue is resolved.

  5. Variance = 3.50

  6. Standard deviation = 1.87

    Squaring in step 4 left the variance in “squared units”. The variance of 10, 20, and 30 is 66.67, which is larger than all of the values. The square root shrinks the answer back down to the original units. The standard deviation of 10, 20, and 30 is 8.16 (much closer to the original values).

The Formula for the Population Standard Deviation

σ= Σ(xi − μ)² N

Mathematically, the formula reads as: sigma equals the square root of the quantity, the sum of open parenthesis x sub i minus mu close parenthesis squared, divided by N.

The formula for the population standard deviation is like a baking recipe. It tells us the steps to follow for a successful outcome. The formula uses symbols rather than words, so that it can be written concisely. On the left side of the equal sign is the lowercase sigma, which looks like the letter ‘o’ with a small tail on its top extending slightly to the right. The lowercase sigma represents the population standard deviation.

On the right side of the equal sign are the steps for calculating the population standard deviation. They tell us to subtract the mean from each value, written as “xi − μ”. The x sub i represents each value. The μ represents the population mean. Next, the formula says, “(xi − μ)²”, telling us to square the difference scores. Then, to the left of those parentheses, sits the uppercase sigma “Σ”, which tells us to ‘add them all up’. All of this sits above a horizontal line, and below is an uppercase N, telling us to divide the sum of the squared difference scores by N (the population size). At this point, we've calculated the variance. Finally, the square root sign encompasses our equation, telling us that sigma (the population standard deviation) equals the square root of the average of the squared difference scores (the variance).
